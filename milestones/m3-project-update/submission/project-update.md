# Project update

- **Current title:** Association between Income and Depression in NHANES Adults
- **Audience:** Staff in a municipal public health department preparing an internal briefing on health equity.
- **Refined question:** How is family income-to-poverty ratio associated with depression symptom scores among adults aged 18 and older in NHANES 2021–2023?
- **Data and analytic sample:** We used the NHANES August 2021–August 2023 depression screener and demographics files. The analysis includes 4,831 adults aged 18 and older with complete information for the variables used in the analysis. Survey weights are now applied to the estimates.
- **Stable finding:** The survey-weighted results continue to show a downward pattern in depression symptom scores as family income-to-poverty ratio increases. Mean PHQ-9 scores were highest in the lowest income group and lowest in the highest income group.
- **Choice needing review:** We would like feedback on whether our use of the NHANES survey design and weights is appropriate for the way we are presenting the results.
