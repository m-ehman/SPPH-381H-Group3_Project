## Project and team
Title: Income and Depression Symptom Patterns in NHANES Adults
Group: Red Team - Yoon Park, Humnah Rashid, Jade Lee, Morgan Ehman

## Audience and KT purpose
We are writing for staff in a municipal public health department who are preparing an internal briefing on health equity. Our knowledge translation (KT) framing is that the data can point to a pattern between income and depressive symptoms that is worth discussing, while making clear it is not evidence of cause. The goal is to help staff judge whether the pattern merits a fuller analysis using the NHANES survey weights, not to guide a policy decision on its own.

## Questions
Primary question: How is family income-to-poverty ratio associated with PHQ-9 depression symptom scores among adults aged 18 and older in NHANES 2021–2023?

Secondary question: How does the association differ across age groups?

## Data and planned output
We will use two linked CDC/NCHS public-use files from NHANES August 2021–August 2023: the depression screener (DPQ_L) and demographics (DEMO_L). The planned outputs are a Table 1-style summary, one family income-to-poverty ratio-by-depression symptom figure, one age-stratified family income-to-poverty ratio-by-depression symptom figure, and a short Quarto report. 

## Known limitations
The PHQ-9 is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. Family income-to-poverty ratio variable is missing for some participants. The survey is cross-sectional, so it cannot show cause. Unweighted results describe the sample only, not the U.S. population.
